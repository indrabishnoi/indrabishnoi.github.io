One of the earliest and most important social bonds for many mammals is the bond formed with their mother. Mothers provide essential benefits for offspring development and survival, but what happens to this bond when animals gain independence during the juvenile period immediately following weaning?

In this research, we investigated whether juvenile mice (postnatal day 26) retain the ability to recognize and prefer their mothers post-weaning. We further examined the strength of this bond using an acute immune activator, lipopolysaccharide (LPS), to understand how immune challenges might affect social preferences.

## The Agora Maze SocioBox Method

This study employed the innovative **Agora Maze SocioBox method**, a multi-chamber social behavior task that allows for nuanced assessment of complex social recognition. The apparatus consists of a central open arena connected to multiple compartments, each housing different stimulus animals or objects. This setup enables researchers to observe how subject mice interact with various social and non-social stimuli simultaneously, providing a more comprehensive understanding of social behavior dynamics.

The Agora Maze SocioBox method has been highlighted as a central tool for studying social behavior in juvenile mice. You can read more about this methodology in this [research alert from Ugo Basile](https://ugobasile.com/blog/16-research-alert/147-studying-social-behavior-in-juvenile-mice-the-central-role-of-the-ugo-basile-agora-sociobox-method).

## Key Findings

Our findings reveal that juvenile mice exhibit a **strong maternal preference**, which was significantly greater than chance and higher compared to any other social or non-social stimuli. The five-chamber social preference task (the AGORA) contained:
- The biological mother
- A sex- and age-matched novel mouse
- A sex- and age-matched sibling
- A novel object
- An empty chamber

While LPS exposure reduced the time spent investigating all stimuli, **juvenile maternal preference was not significantly altered by LPS exposure**, demonstrating the resilience of this social bond. These effects were especially pronounced in females, while subtle shifts towards novel exploration began to emerge in males by postnatal day 26.

## Implications

The novel multi-chamber task employed in this study offered a more nuanced understanding of how social bonds evolve and vary across sex. The current findings suggest that juvenile mice have a robust social preference for their mother that is resilient to early-life immune activation, highlighting the importance of maternal bonds even after weaning.
